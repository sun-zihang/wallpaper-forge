Why transcode in the browser

This runs FFmpeg compiled to WebAssembly: decoding, encoding and muxing all happen on your machine, so your footage never leaves your computer. No queue, no watermark, no account — open a tab, finish the job, close it.

Videos over 300 seconds, or need GPU acceleration and batch automation? The desktop version lifts both the duration and size limits.

FAQ

Do I need to install software to convert WebM to MP4?

No. The transcoding engine (FFmpeg WebAssembly) runs in your browser — open the page and start converting.

Are my files uploaded?

No. Decoding and encoding both happen in your local browser process; the server only delivers the web page itself.

Will conversion noticeably reduce quality?

You control quality: CRF 0-51, defaulting to 23 — close to visually lossless for its size. Raise the CRF or cap the frame rate when you need a smaller file.

How long and how large a video is supported?

Up to 100MB and 300 seconds per file in the web version, validated up front with a plain-language hint; longer or larger videos belong to the desktop version.
